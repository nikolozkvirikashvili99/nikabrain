nikabrain · learning playbook

Learn fast the way giga did,
as a frontend dev

Real work is the classroom. A picture first. Quizzes until you can predict it. You write the core. Nothing counts until it's proven.

1 · the weekly loop

One loop, every week

Each turn starts from a real task and ends with a 2-minute /retro. The retro picks the next concept, so the loop steers itself.

2 · the concept ladder

Reading it is only "seen"

Every concept in me/concepts.md sits on a step. It only goes up with evidence you can point at. This is what stopped giga fooling himself.

3 · patterns that made him faster

12 habits worth stealing

Each one showed up in giga's real sessions. Filter by type.

01

One analogy, then the real thing

Networking clicked through a single building analogy; after that he reasoned on his own.

"Render = taking a photo. Commit = printing only what changed."

02

Predict, then reveal

Guess what happens before you're shown. A wrong guess sticks better than a right answer read off the page.

03

Spaced review

Missed questions come back after 1, 3, 7, 21, 60 days. Five minutes beats an hour of rereading.

04

You type the core

His own audit: "hasn't coded in any logged session". Claude writing everything feels fast and teaches nothing.

05

The bug is the lesson

His best lessons came from things that broke at work. Today's bug is the hook question of the next lesson.

06

Done-line in the first prompt

His biggest measured win: 10% → 62% of prompts. Without it Claude picks the finish line.

07

"Are you 100% sure?"

Turned guesses into 3-minute measurements, and changed answers. Also: "how did you assume that?"

08

Bugs in four parts

Expected · actual · what changed · my guess. "What changed" found bugs fastest.

09

A reviewer catches what tests don't

Background reviews found a permission hole and leaked URLs, with every test green. For UI: ui-reviewer.

10

Paste the real error

Two wasted commits came from guessing what CI wanted. Give Claude the gate's text.

11

Claude knows facts; you bring judgement

414 graded runs: plain Claude got 159/162 knowledge questions. The gain came from process: reproduce first, review.

12

Use before you collect

54 skills installed, 0 used in real work. Rule: add knowledge only when a real task needs it.

7 · what not to copy

Giga's mistakes, so you skip them

×

Collecting instead of using

Hundreds of exam questions and lessons sat untouched for weeks. Build one only when a task needs it.

×

Claude codes everything

Concepts stayed at "seen" until he built the step himself.

×

Long runs with no stop rule

$54 for a 5% gain. Give big jobs a budget and a done-line.

×

Asking for certainty five times

One scope rule in the first prompt ("every caller, all repos") replaced five rebuilds.
